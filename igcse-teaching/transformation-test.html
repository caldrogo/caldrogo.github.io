Geometric Transformations: A Simple Guide

When you change a shape's position or size on a grid, you perform a transformation. There are four main types. Define your shape by dragging the blue points in the grid below, then select a transformation to practice. Use the "Show/Hide" button to check your answers!

1. Translation (Sliding)

This is when you move a shape to a new position without turning or flipping it. You follow a vector .

Example: \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{3} \\ \mathbf{-4} \end{pmatrix}

  • The top number 3 tells you to move 3 units right. (If it was -3, you would move left.)
  • The bottom number -4 tells you to move 4 units down. (If it was 4, you would move up.)

2. Reflection (Flipping)

This creates a mirror image of the shape across a line of reflection (the mirror line).

Example: Reflect across the y-axis (x=0)

  • Step 1: Choose a point, for example, A(2, 5). It is 2 units from the y-axis.
  • Step 2: Count 2 units on the other side of the Y-axis. The new point is A'(-2, 5).
  • Rule: The distance from the point to the mirror line must be the same as the distance from the line to the image.

3. Rotation (Turning)

This is when you turn a shape around a fixed center of rotation. You need an angle and a direction (Clockwise or Anti-Clockwise).

Example: 90 degrees Clockwise about the origin (0, 0)

  • Step 1: Draw a line from the center of rotation to one point on the shape.
  • Step 2: Turn that line by 90 degrees in the Clockwise direction.

4. Enlargement (Resizing)

This changes the size of the shape. You need a center of enlargement and a scale factor (k).

Example: Scale Factor k=2 and Center (0, 0)

  • Step 1: Draw a line from the Center of Enlargement to a point A(1, 4).
  • Step 2: Multiply the coordinates by the scale factor: A'(1 \times 2, 4 \times 2) = A'(2, 8).
  • Rule: The image point is k times further away from the center than the original point. If k is negative, the image is on the opposite side of the center.
